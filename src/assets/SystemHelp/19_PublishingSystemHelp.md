# 19. Publishing System Help

## 01. Content Strategy Help

Content Strategy sets the direction for everything you publish — what it should achieve and who it's actually for.
Define your content goals, the categories of content you'll create, your target audience, and how you'll measure success.
Tie each goal to a clear purpose, like "Community Building — encourage interaction with fans," rather than a generic list of buzzwords.
Getting this right first is what keeps every piece of content you produce actually working toward something.

## 02. Content Ideas Help

Content Ideas is your holding area for anything worth publishing eventually, captured before it's scheduled.
For each idea, record a title, category, status (new, researching, ready, archived), and any notes.
Keep ideas at different stages of readiness side by side, from a brand-new concept to one already approved and ready to schedule.
This is what keeps good ideas from getting lost between when they occur to you and when there's time to actually write them.

## 03. Editorial Calendar Help

The Editorial Calendar turns your ready ideas into an actual publishing schedule.
For each item, set a publication date, category, platform, and status, so everyone can see what's coming and when.
Be honest about where each piece actually stands, mixing Scheduled, Draft, Planned, and Researching rather than treating the whole calendar as fixed.
This is what keeps publishing coordinated across teams instead of relying on individual memory.

## 04. Article Template Help

The Article Template gives every piece of written content the same reliable structure, so readers know what to expect.
Define what belongs in each section: title, introduction, main content, conclusion, and references if needed.
Explain the purpose of each section, not just its name — the conclusion, for instance, exists to "summarize key points and reinforce the message."
A consistent structure like this is what makes your content easier to write, edit, and read.

## 05. Review Workflow Help

Review Workflow defines how content actually gets checked before it goes live, so quality doesn't depend on who happens to be reviewing it.
List your review areas (accuracy, clarity, structure, grammar, formatting, and similar) and describe the approval process step by step.
Lay out approval as a clear sequence — draft to editorial review to subject matter review to final approval — so everyone knows what stage content is at.
This is what catches errors and inconsistencies before your audience does.

## 06. Distribution Checklist Help

The Distribution Checklist makes sure published content actually reaches its audience and that you learn from how it performs.
Confirm publication steps, distribution across channels, and that performance tracking is in place.
Don't stop at publishing — also check that analytics are enabled and that results get reviewed against the content goals set earlier.
This is the step that closes the loop, turning one published piece into a lesson for the next one.
