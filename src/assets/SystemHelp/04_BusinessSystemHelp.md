# 04. Business System Help

## 01. Business Plan Help

The Business Plan is a high-level snapshot of your whole business — what you do, who you serve, how you make money, and what matters most right now.
Cover five things briefly: an organization overview, your products and services, your primary customers, how revenue is generated, and your current business priorities.
Keep each part tight and specific, listing exact product categories and priorities like "expand repair and refurbishment services" instead of generic ambitions.
Treat this as the summary someone should be able to read first to understand your business at a glance.

## 02. Business Model Help

The Business Model explains how the pieces of your business fit together to create, deliver, and capture value.
Define your value proposition, who receives it (customer segments), how it reaches them (delivery channels), and what resources, activities, and partnerships make it possible.
Treat these as connected, not separate — resources, activities, and partners should all clearly support the same value proposition rather than reading as unrelated lists.
This is the map that everything else in this System — revenue, pricing, products — should stay consistent with.

## 03. Revenue Model Help

The Revenue Model explains the mechanics of how money actually comes in — the payment structures behind your Revenue Streams.
List the revenue methods available to you (one-time purchase, subscription, services, and similar), identify which ones you actually prioritize, and estimate how much each contributes.
Make the mix concrete with percentages, like 85% from product sales and 7% from repair services, rather than a vague list of possibilities.
This shows at a glance how dependent your business is on any single revenue method.

## 04. Revenue Matrix Help

Revenue Streams identify the actual sources your money comes from — where the Revenue Model's payment methods get applied.
List each distinct stream with a short description of what it covers.
Keep each stream clearly separate and easy to distinguish, like "premium outdoor apparel" versus "repair and refurbishment services," rather than overlapping categories.
Together with the Revenue Model, this shows both where your money comes from and how it's collected.

## 05. Product Register Help

The Product Register is your complete, at-a-glance list of everything you sell — products and services alike.
For each item, record its name, category, current status, target customer, and which Revenue Stream it feeds.
Be honest about status, including "Planned" items alongside active ones, so the register reflects reality rather than only finished offerings.
Keeping this current gives you one place to see your entire commercial portfolio.

## 06. Customer Profiles Help

Customer Profiles describe the different groups of people your business actually serves, so decisions about products, pricing, and marketing are grounded in who you're serving.
For each customer group, capture their needs, the challenges they face, and what they're ultimately trying to achieve.
Keep each group distinct, separating "environmentally conscious consumers" from "outdoor professionals" even though both buy similar products.
These profiles are what keep your later marketing and product decisions grounded in real people, not assumptions.

## 07. Competitor Analysis Help

Competitor Analysis maps out the alternatives your customers could choose instead of you, so you understand your actual market position.
For each competitor, note their strengths, weaknesses, and what differentiates them, then describe your own market position relative to all of them.
Don't try to beat competitors on their own terms — define a different basis for competing entirely, such as durability and trust instead of price.
Use this section to be honest about competitors' real strengths, not just their weaknesses.

## 08. Pricing Strategy Help

Pricing Strategy defines the logic behind your prices, so pricing decisions stay consistent as you grow rather than being made case by case.
Choose a pricing approach (budget, competitive, premium, value-based), list the principles that guide it, and describe any pricing tiers you offer.
Make your principles actual rules, like "avoid unnecessary discounting," not general statements, so they can actually be checked against a real pricing decision.
This keeps your pricing aligned with your positioning instead of drifting toward whatever the market pressures you into.

## 09. Revenue Roadmap Help

The Revenue Roadmap shows how you expect your revenue to evolve over time, phase by phase.
For each phase, set a time period, a single focus, and the revenue sources you expect to be active during it.
Show a clear progression, from strengthening the core product line in years 1–2 to circular business initiatives by year 9+, with each phase building on the last.
This gives your Business System the same forward-looking structure as your Strategic Roadmap, focused specifically on how revenue will grow.
