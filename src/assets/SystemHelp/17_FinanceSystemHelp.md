# 17. Finance System Help

## 01. Financial Model Help

The Financial Model documents how your organization actually generates and sustains money — the foundation the rest of this System builds on.
List your revenue streams, funding sources, financial objectives, and the principles that guide financial decisions.
Tie each revenue stream and funding source to a clear purpose, like "Research Grants — innovation and technology development," rather than a generic list of income types.
Getting this right first is what keeps your budgeting and planning grounded in how money actually moves through the organization.

## 02. Financial Resources Help

Financial Resources documents how money actually gets spent and controlled, from cost categories to who can approve what.
Cover your cost categories, operating expenses, capital investment, procurement guidelines, expense approval process, and financial policies.
Get specific about approval thresholds, spelling out exactly which expense values require which level of sign-off, rather than leaving approval authority vague.
Clear controls here are what prevent spending from drifting away from what leadership actually approved.

## 03. Budgeting Help

Budgeting documents how financial resources are actually planned and allocated across the organization.
Define your budget categories, how allocation decisions get made, the approval process, and how often budgets get reviewed.
Lay out the approval process as a clear path — department to finance review to executive leadership — rather than leaving it ambiguous who signs off.
A regular review schedule, monthly and quarterly, is what keeps budgets responsive instead of fixed for a year regardless of what changes.

## 04. Financial Planning Help

Financial Planning documents how you prepare for future financial needs and opportunities, not just how you manage today's budget.
Set your financial goals, investment priorities, growth plans, reserve policy, and overall funding strategy.
Separate goals by time horizon — short-term operational performance versus long-term strategic investment — rather than treating all goals the same.
Planning ahead like this is what lets you respond to opportunities and downturns without scrambling.

## 05. Financial Monitoring Help

Financial Monitoring documents how you actually track financial performance on an ongoing basis, not just at year-end.
List the indicators you track (revenue, cash flow, operating costs, budget variance, and similar), your review schedule, and how you analyze performance.
Tie each indicator to a clear purpose — use Budget Variance, for instance, specifically to "compare actual spending against approved budgets."
Regular monitoring like this is what catches financial problems while there's still time to act on them.

## 06. Financial Risk Help

Financial Risk documents the financial threats your organization actually faces and how you'd handle them.
List your financial risks, evaluate each one's likelihood and impact, define mitigation measures, and prepare contingency plans.
Pair every risk with a specific mitigation and a separate contingency plan — for instance, hedging currency exposure day-to-day but revising pricing and forecasts if exchange rates move sharply.
Preparing this in advance is what turns a financial shock into a manageable situation instead of a crisis.

## 07. Financial Review Help

Financial Review is how your whole financial approach stays effective over time — a regular look back at what's actually happening.
Set a review schedule, define what the review evaluates (revenue growth, costs, budget performance, and similar), and list improvement actions.
Review at multiple cadences, from monthly operational reviews up to an annual strategic review, so both short-term issues and long-term trends get caught.
This is what keeps your Financial Model and Planning grounded in reality rather than assumptions that go unchecked.
