# 13. Asset System Help

## 01. Assets Help

The Assets section is your central inventory of every reusable resource worth tracking, from templates to datasets to equipment.
For each asset, record its name, type, purpose, where it's stored, who's responsible for it, its priority, and its current status.
Be specific about ownership and location for each one, like a "Performance Benchmark Dataset" owned by the Performance Engineering Team and stored in a named repository, not just a general description.
Naming a responsible owner for each asset is what keeps it actually maintained instead of forgotten.

## 02. Access Help

Access records who can actually use each asset and under what conditions, so sensitive resources stay protected without becoming inaccessible to the people who need them.
For each asset, set an access level (available, limited, restricted), note any restrictions, and add relevant notes.
Explain restrictions with real reasons, like requiring approval for a dataset because it "contains internal performance information," rather than restricting access without explanation.
This is what balances making assets reusable with keeping sensitive resources properly controlled.

## 03. Updates Help

Updates tracks the maintenance history of each asset, so you know what's changed and what's still due.
For each entry, note which asset was affected, what was updated, the date, and its status (planned, completed, deferred).
Record both completed work and planned future updates, such as an already-finished accessibility improvement alongside a benchmark expansion still planned for later.
Keeping this current is what prevents assets from quietly becoming outdated.

## 04. Summary Help

The Assets Summary gives a quick, high-level view of your whole asset inventory, without needing to read every individual record.
List your key assets, note which are currently active, flag update priorities, add one Key Takeaway, and set your next review date.
Make the Key Takeaway a real point, such as centralizing assets improving consistency and speed, rather than just repeating the lists above.
This is the section to check first when deciding where maintenance attention should go next.
