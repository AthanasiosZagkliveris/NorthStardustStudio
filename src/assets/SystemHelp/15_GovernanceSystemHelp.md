# 15. Governance System Help

## 01. Governance Framework Help

Governance Framework establishes who has authority to decide what, and how accountability actually works across your organization.
Define your governance principles, map responsibilities by role, clarify who has authority over which types of decisions, and describe your accountability structure and objectives.
Tie decision authority directly to decision type — for instance, "major investments" requiring Executive Leadership while "daily operational decisions" stay with Department Managers — rather than leaving authority vague.
This is the foundation every other section in this System depends on.

## 02. Policies & Standards Help

Policies & Standards documents the actual rules and expectations that guide behavior and decisions across the organization.
List your organizational policies, internal standards, code of conduct, ethical principles, and how policies get reviewed over time.
Name real, specific policies, like an Anti-Corruption Policy and Cybersecurity Standards, rather than a generic mention of "having policies."
A defined review process is what keeps these from becoming outdated documents nobody actually follows.

## 03. Compliance Help

Compliance documents how your organization actually meets its legal, regulatory, and contractual obligations.
List the requirements that apply to you, who's responsible for compliance, the activities that support it, how it's monitored, and how issues get corrected.
Split responsibility clearly across roles, such as Legal Department for interpretation and Business Units for daily implementation, rather than treating compliance as one team's job.
This section is your evidence that governance translates into actual practice, not just policy.

## 04. Risk Management Help

Risk Management documents the risks your organization actually faces and how you handle them, before they become real problems.
List your risk categories, assess each risk's likelihood and impact, define how you'll treat it (avoid, reduce, transfer, accept), and describe how risk is monitored and reviewed.
Rate risks honestly and specifically, marking cybersecurity as High likelihood and High impact rather than treating every risk the same.
This is what lets you focus attention on the risks that could actually hurt you most.

## 05. Information Security Help

Information Security documents how you actually protect the organization's sensitive information and digital resources.
List your important information assets, how access is controlled, how data is protected, the security measures in place, and your incident response process.
Lay out incident response as clear, ordered steps — detect, assess, contain, recover, investigate, improve — rather than a vague promise to "respond appropriately."
Documenting this in advance is what determines how quickly and effectively you can react to a real security incident.

## 06. Business Continuity Help

Business Continuity documents how you'll keep essential operations running, and recover, if something seriously disrupts the organization.
Identify your critical activities, your continuity plans, recovery procedures, how you'll communicate during a disruption, and how you'll review and test all of this.
Don't just plan for recovery — plan for communication too, naming a crisis communication team and the specific groups to notify.
Testing this regularly, through simulation exercises, is what ensures the plan actually works when you need it, not just on paper.

## 07. Governance Review Help

Governance Review is how the whole System stays effective over time — a regular check on whether your governance practices are actually working.
Set a review schedule, define what the review evaluates, and list improvement actions coming out of it.
Review governance at multiple cadences — monthly reporting, quarterly executive review, and an annual full assessment — rather than a single once-a-year check.
This is what keeps governance a living practice instead of a document that's written once and forgotten.
