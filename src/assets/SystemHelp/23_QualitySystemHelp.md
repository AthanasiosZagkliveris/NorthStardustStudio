# 23. Quality System Help

## 01. Quality Standards Help

Quality Standards defines what "acceptable quality" actually means in each area of your organization.
For each quality area (products, services, projects, documentation, and similar), state the standard and who's responsible for it.
Make standards checkable, like "products meet defined quality specifications before delivery," rather than a vague commitment to quality.
This is the baseline every other section in this System measures against.

## 02. Quality Objectives Help

Quality Objectives sets measurable goals for improving quality in each area, beyond just maintaining the standard.
For each quality area, state the objective and the success criteria that would show it's been met.
Pair each objective with criteria you could actually check ("reduce product defects" measured by "continuous reduction in defects," for instance), not just a wish to do better.
These objectives are what give your quality efforts direction rather than just monitoring the status quo.

## 03. Quality Controls Help

Quality Controls documents the actual activities that verify your standards are being met, before problems reach customers.
For each quality area, define the control activity, how often it happens, and who's responsible.
Match frequency to risk (daily process inspections for manufacturing versus quarterly documentation reviews) rather than checking everything on the same schedule.
These controls are what catch quality issues early instead of discovering them after the fact.

## 04. Quality Reviews Help

Quality Reviews documents how you step back and evaluate quality across the organization, not just check individual controls.
Set a review schedule, the areas you'll cover, and the methods you'll use (inspections, audits, customer feedback, and similar).
Review at multiple cadences (daily production checks up to an annual full quality system review), combining several review methods rather than relying on just one.
This is what surfaces patterns that individual quality controls alone would miss.

## 05. Quality Findings Help

Quality Findings records the actual issues discovered during reviews, so they don't just get noticed and forgotten.
For each finding, describe it, note its impact, and assign a responsible role.
Also define priority levels (low, medium, high) so findings can be triaged by how urgently they need attention rather than treated identically.
This record is what becomes the basis for the improvement actions in the next section.

## 06. Quality Improvement Help

Quality Improvement turns your findings into specific actions that actually change how things work.
For each action, describe it, assign a responsible role, set a target completion, and track its review status (planned, in progress, ongoing, completed).
Tie every action directly to a finding, such as strengthening supplier quality reviews in response to material variation issues, rather than generic improvement goals.
This is what makes quality management a continuous cycle instead of a one-time assessment.
