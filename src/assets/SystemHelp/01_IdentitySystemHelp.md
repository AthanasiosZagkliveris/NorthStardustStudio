# 01. Identity System Help

## 01. Mission Help

Your Mission is a short, plain statement of why your organization exists — not a slogan, but a working answer to "why do we exist?"
Keep it to 1–3 sentences (roughly 20–75 words), covering what you do, who you do it for, and why that work matters.
Aim to tie all three together in one sentence: what's made, who it serves, and the deeper purpose behind it, so the whole thing reads as a single clear thread.
Get this right first: your Vision, Values, and Positioning all build directly on it.

## 02. Vision Help

Your Vision describes the future you're working toward if your Mission succeeds — not what you do today, but the change you want to help create.
Keep it to 1–3 sentences (roughly 20–75 words), and make it ambitious and inspiring rather than operational.
Think about what success looks like years from now, and let the vision point beyond your product or service to the broader change you want your work to bring about.
This gives your later Values and Principles something bigger to point toward.

## 03. Values Help

Values are the beliefs that guide your decisions and behavior — what you prioritize and refuse to compromise on.
List 3–7 values, each with a short explanation of what it actually means day to day.
Keep each value concrete rather than abstract — pair a word like "Simplicity" with a short explanation such as "favor practical, effective solutions over unnecessary complexity."
These values become the standard your Principles and Brand Personality will build on.

## 04. Philosophy Help

Philosophy explains how your organization thinks, not just what it does — the reasoning and beliefs behind your decisions.
Write 1–5 short paragraphs (roughly 100–500 words) covering the ideas that shape how you approach your work and challenges.
Avoid restating your mission here — explain the reasoning behind it instead, connecting ideas like growth, quality, and impact into a coherent line of thinking.
Use this to give context to every principle and decision that follows.

## 05. Principles Help

Principles turn your Values into practical rules for action — Values describe what you believe, Principles describe how you actually behave and decide.
Aim for 5–15 principles, each with a short explanation of what it means in practice.
Make each principle concrete and actionable, like "Repair Before Replace," rather than a value restated in different words.
These become standards your team can actually apply when facing a decision or evaluating an opportunity.

## 06. Manifesto Help

Your Manifesto is a public, persuasive statement of what your organization believes and stands for — more emotional and declarative than your Mission or Vision.
Aim for 300–1,500 words, optionally structured around an introduction, your core beliefs, the challenges you see, what you stand for, and your commitment to the future.
Short, direct contrasts build momentum well, like "durability over disposability" or "repair over replacement" — write in that same conviction-driven voice.
This is the version of your identity meant to be shared publicly, on your website, in brand materials, or with your team.

## 07. Brand Personality Help

Brand Personality gives your organization human characteristics — if your brand were a person, how would it act, and what impression would it leave?
Pick 3–7 traits, each with a short explanation of what it means in practice — for instance, pairing "Humble" with "let actions speak louder than promotion."
These traits should describe how people should experience and describe you, not just words you'd like to be true.
This consistency carries directly into your Voice Guide and every customer-facing interaction.

## 08. Voice Guide Help

The Voice Guide defines how your organization sounds when it communicates — the tone behind your Brand Personality.
List 3–7 voice characteristics, each with a short explanation, and note anything to avoid — things like fear-based messaging, greenwashing, or empty marketing claims.
Think about how you want to sound, and just as importantly, how you don't want to sound.
A clear voice keeps every piece of writing — from your website to social media — feeling like it comes from the same organization.

## 09. Positioning Statement Help

Your Positioning Statement explains how you want to be understood in your market — who you serve, what you offer, and what sets you apart.
Keep it to 1–3 sentences (roughly 25–100 words), naming your audience, your offer, and your differentiator clearly.
A useful pattern to follow is "for [audience], we provide [offer], while [differentiator]" — shape yours around that structure.
This statement should tie directly back to your Mission and Values so it reads as consistent with everything else in this System.

## 10. Organization Description Help

This section asks for three ready-to-use summaries of your organization at different lengths: Short (10–25 words) for bios and directory listings, Standard (40–100 words) for websites and press intros, and Extended (150–300 words) for about pages and media kits.
Write each version to stand alone — someone should be able to use just the Short version without ever seeing the others.
Let the same core message scale up smoothly across all three, adding more of the "why" and "how" as the format allows.
Having all three ready means you'll never have to improvise a description under pressure.
