# 20. Website System Help

## 01. Website Specification Help

Website Specification defines your website at a high level before any pages get built — why it exists, who it's for, and what it needs to cover.
State the website's purpose, target audience, goals (inform, educate, generate leads, sell products, and similar), and scope.
Make the scope concrete and complete, listing every part of the digital ecosystem from the corporate site to investor relations, rather than a vague description.
Getting this right first is what keeps every later section, architecture, pages, SEO, aligned with the same purpose.

## 02. Content Architecture Help

Content Architecture defines how information is organized across your website, so visitors can actually find what they're looking for.
List your main sections, describe how visitors navigate between them, and map out the page hierarchy.
Keep navigation consistent across a large, multi-site ecosystem, using the same primary structure everywhere rather than letting each section develop its own pattern.
Getting this right is what keeps a growing website usable instead of turning into a maze.

## 03. Website Pages Help

Website Pages is your complete inventory of every page the site needs, with its purpose and what goes on it.
For each page, record its name, purpose, key content, and status (planned, in development, published, archived).
Tie every page to a specific job — the Careers page, for instance, exists for "recruitment" with "open positions and culture" as its content — rather than a vague placeholder.
This inventory is what keeps development organized as the site grows past just a handful of pages.

## 04. Search Engine Optimization Plan Help

The SEO Plan defines how people will actually find your website through search, rather than leaving discoverability to chance.
Identify your target keywords, then cover both content optimization (titles, meta descriptions, headings, internal links) and technical optimization (sitemaps, mobile performance, structured data).
Tie each practice to a specific outcome, such as image alt text existing to "improve accessibility and search indexing," not just a checkbox to complete.
Covering both content and technical sides is what actually moves your search visibility, not just one or the other.

## 05. Analytics Plan Help

The Analytics Plan defines how you'll actually know whether your website is working, not just that it's live.
List your success metrics, the tools you'll use to measure them, and how often you'll report on performance.
Set a different reporting cadence for different needs — weekly performance checks against a quarterly executive dashboard — rather than one single reporting rhythm for everything.
This is what turns your website from something you launched into something you actively improve.

## 06. Website Roadmap Help

The Website Roadmap lays out how your website will develop over time, phase by phase, beyond the initial launch.
For each phase, define the time period, its focus, and the deliverables expected by the end of it.
Move logically from foundational pages, to content expansion, to user experience improvements, to ongoing optimization, with each phase building on the last.
This is what keeps website development purposeful instead of an endless list of unprioritized changes.
