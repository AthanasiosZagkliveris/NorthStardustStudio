# 11. Research System Help

## 01. Research Overview Help

The Research Overview sets the scope of your research before you start collecting anything — what you're studying and what you're trying to learn.
Name the research, classify its type (market, product, customer, technical, and similar), summarize it briefly, and state a clear objective.
Make the objective specific and decision-oriented, like "determine which AI capabilities provide the greatest business value," rather than an open-ended topic to explore.
A tight objective here keeps every source and finding that follows actually relevant.

## 02. Research Questions Help

Research Questions break your objective down into the specific things you actually need to answer.
List each question, set its priority (critical, important, optional), and track whether it's open, in progress, or answered.
Keep questions genuinely answerable, like "which business activities benefit most from generative AI?" rather than broad topics disguised as questions.
These questions are what keep your research focused instead of collecting information for its own sake.

## 03. Sources Help

Sources records exactly where your information came from, so your research can be checked and trusted.
For each source, name it, classify its type (interview, report, dataset, article, and similar), record a reference or citation, and note what you took from it.
Deliberately mix source types — customer interviews, market reports, internal data, and academic research — rather than relying on just one kind of evidence.
Using a range of source types is what makes your Findings credible rather than one-sided.

## 04. Findings Help

Findings record what you actually discovered — the raw evidence, kept separate from your interpretation of it.
For each finding, state it clearly, link it to the sources that support it, and rate your confidence in it (high, medium, low).
Be honest about confidence levels, marking a finding "Medium" when the evidence is thinner than the others, rather than treating everything as equally certain.
Keeping findings separate from conclusions is what lets your Analysis section interpret the evidence rather than just repeat it.

## 05. Analysis Help

Analysis is where your Findings actually turn into meaning — patterns, relationships, and implications for your decision.
For each analysis, describe the observation, interpret what it means, link it to the findings that support it, and rate your confidence.
Draw a real interpretation, connecting a pattern in customer behavior to what it means for adoption, rather than just restating the finding in different words.
This is the step that turns evidence into something you can actually act on.

## 06. Actions Help

Actions turns your analysis into specific next steps, so the research actually leads somewhere.
For each action, describe what should happen, explain why it's recommended, set a priority, and track its status.
Tie every action directly back to a reason drawn from the analysis, like prioritizing integrated AI features "because customers prefer familiar workflows."
Research that doesn't produce actions like these tends to stay filed away instead of changing anything.

## 07. Research Summary Help

The Research Summary condenses everything above into the version a decision-maker will actually read.
List your key findings, key conclusions, and key actions, then add one Key Takeaway and a clear next step.
Make the Key Takeaway a real claim, like "successful AI adoption depends as much on trust as on technological capability," not a generic wrap-up sentence.
This is the section most people will read first, so make sure it can stand entirely on its own.
