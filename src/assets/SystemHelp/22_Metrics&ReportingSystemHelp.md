# 22. Metrics & Reporting System Help

## 01. Performance Areas Help

Performance Areas identifies the major organizational functions whose performance actually gets measured.
For each area, name it, state its performance objective, and note the source system or process the data comes from.
Tie each area to a real data source, like Manufacturing tracked through the Manufacturing Execution System, rather than an area with no way to actually measure it.
This is the foundation your KPIs in the next section will be built on.

## 02. Key Performance Indicators (KPI) Help

This section turns each Performance Area into something you can actually track — a measurable indicator of progress, not just a general goal.
For every KPI, capture the Performance Area it belongs to, the KPI itself, its target, how often it's measured, and who's responsible for it.
Keep every KPI specific and numeric — tie "Production Efficiency" to a hard target such as 98% measured daily by a named role — so two people would measure it the same way.
This is what makes your later Reporting and Performance Review sections actually usable.

## 03. Reporting Framework Help

Reporting Framework defines the actual reports that communicate performance, and who each one is for.
For each report, name it, state its purpose, its audience, and who prepares it.
Match report detail to audience — a Daily Operations Report for the production team versus a Quarterly Executive Dashboard for leadership — rather than one report trying to serve everyone.
Getting this right is what keeps people from being buried in detail they don't need or missing information they do.

## 04. Reporting Schedule Help

Reporting Schedule sets exactly when each report goes out and how it reaches people, so reporting happens reliably instead of whenever someone remembers.
For each report, set its frequency and distribution method.
Pair frequency with a fitting method, like a Quarterly Executive Dashboard delivered through an executive presentation rather than just an email attachment.
Consistency here is what makes performance information something people can actually count on.

## 05. Performance Review Help

Performance Review is where the numbers actually get discussed, evaluated, and turned into conclusions.
Set your review schedule, the areas you'll cover (finance, operations, customer service, and similar), and what to look for in key findings.
Review at multiple cadences — daily operational reviews up through an annual organizational assessment — so both immediate issues and long-term trends get caught.
This is the step that turns collected data into genuine insight instead of a report nobody discusses.

## 06. Improvement Actions Help

Improvement Actions records what you're actually doing in response to your performance data, so measurement leads to real change.
For each action, describe it, name a responsible role, set a target completion, and track its review status (planned, in progress, ongoing, completed).
Tie every action to a specific performance gap, such as reducing product defects owned by the Quality Manager with a Q3 target, rather than a vague intention to improve.
This is what makes the whole System worthwhile — performance measurement only creates value when it actually leads to action.
