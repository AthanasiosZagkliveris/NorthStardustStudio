# 03. Strategy System Help

## 01. Current Situation Analysis Help

This section captures exactly where your organization stands today — the honest starting point every future plan depends on.
Describe your current state (size, products or services, audience, resources, capabilities), then list the opportunities and challenges you're facing right now.
Don't just describe the business — name specific, real opportunities like "growing demand for sustainable products" and challenges like "higher production costs," rather than vague statements.
Getting this accurate matters more than making it sound good; your SWOT and Strategic Compass both build on it.

## 02. SWOT Analysis Help

SWOT Analysis organizes your internal advantages and limitations alongside external possibilities and risks, into four clear categories.
List specific Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats — each item should be concrete enough to act on, not a generic statement.
Close with a short takeaway connecting the analysis to a real conclusion, such as quality and trust being the true competitive advantage, drawing a similar conclusion from your own list.
This becomes the evidence base for the priorities you set in your Strategic Compass and Goals.

## 03. Strategic Compass Help

The Strategic Compass is your decision-making reference point — the direction every future opportunity should be measured against.
Define your long-term direction, the focus areas that deserve the most attention, the assumptions your strategy depends on, and the filters you'll use to evaluate decisions.
Turn decision filters into direct tests, like "does this reduce unnecessary environmental impact?" so they can be applied as real questions.
Revisit the assumptions section periodically, since your whole strategy depends on them staying true.

## 04. Strategic Themes Help

Strategic Themes are your broad priorities for this planning period — not specific actions yet, just the big areas you're organizing everything else around.
Define a handful of themes, each with a short description.
Keep every theme to a single clear sentence, like "expand circular product lifecycle services" — broad enough to guide multiple goals, specific enough to mean something.
Your Goals, Objectives, and Initiatives should each trace back to one of these themes.

## 05. Goals Help

Goals describe what success actually looks like if your Strategic Themes succeed — ambitious, but still realistic.
Write one goal per theme (or however many apply), each as a clear description of the desired outcome.
Keep every goal outcome-focused, like "expand repair, maintenance, and refurbishment services globally," rather than describing an activity.
These goals are what your Objectives will turn into something measurable.

## 06. Objectives Help

Objectives turn each Goal into something specific and measurable, ideally with a target date attached.
For each objective, note which Goal it supports, describe exactly what will be achieved, and set a target date or timeframe.
Be concrete about timing, like "expand repair services to all major operating regions within two years," and avoid objectives that could be true or false regardless of when you check.
This is what makes your strategy trackable rather than aspirational.

## 07. Key Initiatives Help

Key Initiatives are the actual projects that will make your Objectives happen — this is where strategy turns into execution.
For each initiative, link it to an objective, describe what it involves, name an owner, and note its current status.
Assign clear ownership and honest status labels like "Planned" or "In Progress," rather than leaving accountability vague.
Without a named owner, an initiative tends to stay an idea rather than become work.

## 08. Success Metrics Help

Success Metrics are how you'll know whether the strategy is actually working, not just whether it feels like it's working.
For each metric, describe what's being measured, set a target, and record the current value so progress is visible.
Use real numbers throughout, like 72% against a 90% target, rather than vague direction statements.
Pick metrics that would genuinely change a decision if they moved, not ones that are easy to report but don't matter.

## 09. Strategic Roadmap Help

The Strategic Roadmap lays your strategy out on a timeline, so priorities and expectations are clear across the phases ahead.
For each phase, set a time period, a single focus, and the key deliverables that mark it complete.
Build phases that clearly progress from one to the next, moving from strengthening internal capabilities toward long-term industry leadership.
This is what turns everything above it — themes, goals, objectives, initiatives — into a plan people can actually follow over time.
