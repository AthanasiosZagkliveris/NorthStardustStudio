# 16. People System Help

## 01. Organization Structure Help

Organization Structure maps out how people are actually organized: departments, teams, reporting lines, and where everyone sits.
Document your departments and their purposes, your teams, reporting relationships, how role levels distribute responsibility, and locations if relevant.
Tie each department and team to a clear purpose, like "Automation Engineering: industrial automation projects," rather than just naming groups.
This structure is the reference point everything else in this System, from roles to collaboration, will build on.

## 02. Roles & Responsibilities Help

Roles & Responsibilities documents each role itself, not the person currently in it, so the framework stays valid as people change.
For each role, define its purpose, key responsibilities, authority, reporting line, required qualifications and experience, technical skills, professional competencies, and key deliverables.
Be thorough and specific for every field, right down to "3–5 years in industrial automation" rather than a vague experience requirement.
This level of detail is what makes a role easy to hire for, onboard into, and evaluate against.

## 03. Recruitment Help

Recruitment documents how you actually find, assess, and select new people, so hiring stays consistent and fair across every role.
Define your hiring principles, the candidate profile you're looking for, your recruitment workflow, selection criteria, interview stages, and assessment methods, and clarify who makes the final hiring decision.
Lay out the workflow as clear, ordered steps, from workforce planning through to employment offer, so everyone knows what stage a candidate is at.
Naming who approves the final decision, like a Hiring Manager and HR together, is what keeps hiring accountable rather than left to one person's judgment.

## 04. Onboarding Help

Onboarding documents how a new hire actually becomes a productive, contributing member of the organization.
Cover the welcome process, orientation topics, resources they'll need, a training schedule, and their initial objectives.
Break the schedule into concrete phases (Week 1, Month 1, and the First 90 Days), each with a clear focus, rather than one open-ended ramp-up period.
A structured onboarding like this is what gets new people contributing faster and with fewer early mistakes.

## 05. Development Help

Development documents how people grow their skills and careers over time, not just how they're trained once at the start.
Cover skills development, training options, career development paths, mentoring, and available learning resources.
Offer multiple real paths forward (internal mobility, leadership pathways, and specialist career paths) rather than a single generic promotion track.
Investing in this section is what keeps people improving and staying with the organization long-term.

## 06. Performance Help

Performance documents how contribution and growth actually get evaluated, so expectations are clear and consistent.
Set performance objectives, define your review cadence, describe the feedback process, and list the improvement actions available when needed.
Combine self-assessment with manager feedback and a forward-looking development discussion, not just a one-way evaluation.
This is what turns performance reviews into something that actually helps people improve, not just a formality.

## 07. Collaboration Help

Collaboration documents how people are actually expected to work together, both within teams and across them.
Describe your team collaboration practices, the cross-team relationships that matter most, and the guidelines everyone should follow.
Name the specific cross-team relationships that matter, like "Engineering and Manufacturing," rather than a generic statement about teamwork.
Clear guidelines here, like agreed response times and escalation paths, are what prevent collaboration from depending on individual goodwill alone.

## 08. Offboarding Help

Offboarding documents how someone leaves the organization without leaving gaps behind: in knowledge, responsibilities, or security.
Cover the exit process, knowledge transfer, handover of responsibilities, asset return, access removal, a final documentation review, and exit feedback.
Treat knowledge transfer and handover as seriously as asset return, making sure project continuity and stakeholder notification happen before someone leaves, not after.
A structured offboarding like this protects the organization's continuity and captures honest feedback while it's still fresh.
