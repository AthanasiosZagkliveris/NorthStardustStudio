# Systems Introduction

## 01. Identity System

First, **Definition** sets out what your organization does, its mission, where it stands in the market, and the future it is working towards. Then **Ethics** records your philosophy, your values, the principles you follow, and your manifesto. **Legal Entity** records your legal form, founders and ownership, registration, tax, and running costs, even if you have not registered yet. Finally, **Founders Profile** records who the founders are, their experience and skills, and the gaps in the team and how they are covered. Everything else in the Foundation builds on these answers, so this is the place to start.

## 02. Brand System

First, you imagine your brand as a person, your **Ambassador**: how the Ambassador looks, behaves, and sounds, and the impression the Ambassador leaves. Then **Style** sets your colours, your typefaces and what each is for, and the size of each text level. Finally, **Inventory** gathers your icon, your logo, and the images that show your brand's character. The result is a set of brand standards that anyone working with you can apply the same way.

## 03. Business System

**Foundation** sets out your customer segments and profiles, your competitors, your value propositions, and your offerings. **Customer Engagement** records how customers find you, the relationships you keep with them, and how many sales to expect from each source. **Infrastructure & Capability** lists the founders, resources, activities, and partners you need, and checks that there are enough hours to deliver what you sell. **Financial Foundation** gathers your costs and works out how much funding you need. **Sustainability** turns expected sales into revenue, finds your break-even point, and stress-tests the plan. From your answers, the app writes a Business Plan, a Business Model Canvas, and a Financial Forecast.

## 04. Strategy System

**Situation & Direction** looks at where you are today, your strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats, and your Strategic Compass: the way you will move towards your Vision. It also sets the Strategic Period and the Strategic Themes that frame the plan. **Goals & Objectives** turns each Theme into Goals, and each Goal into Objectives, each with dates, an owner, the Initiatives that deliver it, and the Metrics that show progress. Finally, the **Roadmap** lays every Objective across the Strategic Period, so you can see what happens when and review the plan as things change.

## 05. Client System

The Client System gives you one place to record and manage everything about the people and organizations you serve. It helps you keep track of client needs, the services you are delivering, and the requests coming in, so relationships do not rely on memory or scattered notes. This matters most while you are actively working with clients, since a clear record of who they are and what they need makes every interaction smoother. Once it is filled out, you will have a documented client list, a record of requests, and a summary that gives you real visibility into how those relationships are going.

## 06. Project System

The Project System is where individual pieces of work get organized from start to finish. It keeps track of a project's objectives, scope, resources, deliverables, and progress, so nothing important gets lost once the work actually starts. Use it any time you need to plan effort, manage deadlines, or keep a project moving toward completion. By the end, every project has clear objectives, a defined scope, organized resources, and milestones you can actually measure progress against.

## 07. Product Development System

The Product Development System covers the full journey a product takes, from an early idea all the way through launch and beyond. It helps you evaluate whether an opportunity is worth pursuing, define what the product actually needs to do, plan the work, and prepare properly for launch. Because it spans the whole lifecycle, it is just as useful for improving something you have already released as it is for building something new. Completing it leaves you with a documented product portfolio, clear development priorities, and real insights from what is already shipped that can shape what comes next.

## 08. Operations System

The Operations System documents the recurring work that keeps your organization running day to day. It is where you define processes, assign responsibilities, and record the operational details that make daily work consistent instead of improvised each time. Lean on it whenever recurring tasks need structure, or when you want operations to hold up as the organization grows. What you get is a set of operational standards and standard operating procedures that anyone can follow, along with clearly defined responsibilities for who owns what.

## 09. Innovation System

The Innovation System gives new ideas a place to land before you decide whether they are worth pursuing. It helps you capture ideas as they come up, weigh their potential value, and figure out what to do with them next, without committing resources too early. Turn to it whenever an improvement gets proposed or a new opportunity surfaces and needs a fair evaluation. Over time, it builds into a documented list of ideas and evaluated opportunities, giving you a clear view of what is worth acting on next.

## 10. Documentation System

The Documentation System sets the ground rules for how information gets organized across your organization. It covers folder structures, naming conventions, terminology, and templates, so documents stay easy to find and consistent no matter who created them. Reach for it when documentation feels scattered, or when you want new documents to follow the same standard from the start. Once it is in place, you will have an organized structure, reusable templates, and maintenance rules that keep everything accessible well into the future.

## 11. Research System

The Research System keeps your investigations organized, from the first question you are trying to answer through to the conclusions you draw. It structures how you collect information, evaluate the evidence, and spot patterns, so your research actually holds up when it is used to support a decision. It is especially useful when you are exploring a new topic, comparing alternatives, or need to validate an assumption before committing to it. The result is a defined research objective, organized sources, documented findings, and a summary that turns evidence into a recommended course of action.

## 12. Knowledge System

The Knowledge System is where hard-won experience gets captured instead of staying in one person's head. It gives you a structure for recording lessons learned, best practices, and answers to the questions that come up again and again, so that knowledge is genuinely reusable across the organization. It earns its keep whenever something worth remembering happens, whether that is a lesson from a project or a practice worth standardizing. What builds up over time is a proper knowledge base, complete with documented lessons and FAQs that save people from solving the same problem twice.

## 13. Asset System

The Asset System is your record of the important assets your organization relies on, and where to find them. It tracks what exists, who can access it, and when it was last updated, which matters most for anything shared across multiple projects or teams. It comes into play whenever assets need tracking, maintenance, or a review to confirm they are still accurate and useful. The payoff is a documented inventory and a clear summary that gives everyone visibility into what your organization actually has.

## 14. Data Governance System

The Data Governance System documents how your organization's data is identified, protected, and looked after throughout its life. It exists to keep that data accurate, consistent, secure, and accessible enough to actually support good decisions. You will want it whenever data ownership needs clarifying, quality needs maintaining, or compliance and security need to be put in writing rather than assumed. Completing it gives you a documented data inventory along with clear frameworks for ownership, quality, security, and ongoing review.

## 15. Governance System

The Governance System documents how your organization is actually directed and held accountable, not just on paper but in practice. It covers decision-making structures, policies, assigned authority, and the safeguards that protect the organization when things go wrong. This is the section to open when responsibilities need clarifying, when policies need to be written down, or when governance itself needs a real review. Once complete, you will have documented policies, a compliance framework, a risk management framework, and a plan for keeping the organization running through disruption.

## 16. People System

The People System covers the entire arc of how people contribute to your organization, from the roles you define to how someone eventually moves on. It documents recruitment, onboarding, development, performance, and collaboration, so managing people follows a consistent approach rather than improvising each time. It is worth working through whenever roles need defining, skills need managing, or team responsibilities feel unclear. By the end, you will have a defined people structure and complete frameworks for recruiting, developing, and evaluating everyone who works with you.

## 17. Finance System

The Finance System documents how money moves through your organization: where it comes from, how it is allocated, and how you keep track of both. It exists to support real financial decisions, not just record-keeping, which is why it covers budgeting, planning, monitoring, and risk together. Put it to work whenever you are planning a budget, tracking income and expenses, or need a clearer picture before making a financial call. What you end up with is a documented financial model plus the frameworks for planning, monitoring, and reviewing your finances going forward.

## 18. Stakeholder System

The Stakeholder System keeps track of everyone who has real influence on your organization, or is affected by what it does, from investors to partners to the wider community. It helps you identify these groups, understand what they expect, and plan how you communicate and engage with each of them. Bring it out whenever a stakeholder relationship needs managing more deliberately, or when their expectations are not as clear as they should be. The result is a documented stakeholder register along with frameworks for engagement, communication, and ongoing review.

## 19. Publishing System

The Publishing System organizes everything involved in getting content out into the world, from the first idea to final distribution. It sets standards for creating and reviewing content, and keeps a schedule so publishing happens consistently rather than in bursts. Work through it whenever content needs planning, a publishing schedule needs coordinating, or your review process needs tightening up. What you build here is a documented content strategy, a bank of ideas, and clear procedures for creating, reviewing, and distributing everything you publish.

## 20. Website System

The Website System covers your website from initial planning through to ongoing improvement. It helps you define what the site needs to do, organize its structure and content, and set up ways to measure how well it is actually performing. Rely on it whenever you are building a new site, reorganizing content, or planning improvements based on real performance data. Once complete, you will have a full website specification, a page inventory, and a roadmap that guides how the site develops from here.

## 21. Social Media System

The Social Media System brings structure to how your organization shows up across social platforms. It covers defining your audience, building a strategy for each platform, planning content, and tracking how campaigns and posts actually perform. Call on it whenever you are scheduling content, launching a campaign, or trying to bring more consistency to how often and how well you post. By the end, you will have defined audience profiles, platform strategies, a posting schedule, and tracking procedures that show what is actually working.

## 22. Metrics & Reporting System

The Metrics & Reporting System is where you decide what actually gets measured across the organization, and how those numbers turn into reports people use. It helps you define meaningful performance indicators, build a reporting schedule, and set up a review process so results get discussed rather than just collected. Make use of it whenever performance needs measuring, indicators need defining, or reporting feels inconsistent from one period to the next. What comes out of it is a set of defined performance areas, real KPIs, and a reporting and review structure that keeps decisions grounded in actual data.

## 23. Quality System

The Quality System documents what "good" actually means across your products, services, and operations, and how you make sure you keep delivering it. It covers setting quality standards, running controls that catch issues early, and reviewing results so problems get addressed rather than repeated. Pull it out whenever standards need defining, quality needs monitoring, or you are formalizing how issues get identified and resolved. Completing it gives you documented standards, a quality control framework, and a review process that turns quality into something you actively manage instead of just hoping for.

## 24. Improvement System

The Improvement System is where every opportunity to get better, wherever it comes from, gets tracked from first idea through to lasting change. It helps you prioritize which improvements matter most, implement the ones you approve, and keep checking afterward that they are still delivering value. Come back to it whenever an improvement opportunity surfaces, or when you want a repeatable process instead of ad hoc fixes. By the end, you will have an improvement register, a prioritization framework, and a monitoring process that keeps your organization getting better on purpose, not by accident.
