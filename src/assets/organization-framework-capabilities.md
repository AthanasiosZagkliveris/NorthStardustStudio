# Organization Framework: Capability Descriptions

The framework is built on six capabilities. Each covers a part of running an organization that most teams handle informally until informal stops working. The order matters, but this is not a checklist you finish. The last capability feeds back into the first.

---

## 01. Foundation
*Developing organizational alignment*

Every organization runs on a handful of core decisions. Who we are, what we stand for, where we are heading, and how we create value. Most companies never write them down, so they live in the heads of two or three people. That works until the team grows and the founders are no longer in every conversation. Then two people describe the company two different ways, priorities shift for reasons nobody can explain, and the work slowly drifts from the intent behind it.

Foundation is where those decisions get made on purpose and put in writing. It is built from four connected systems. Identity sets your core principles, Brand expresses them consistently, Business applies them to how you make money, and Strategy points your long-term direction at them. Together they give the team a shared reference to check decisions against, so people can act independently without pulling in different directions.

**Core principle:** Every organization has a foundation. The difference is whether it has been intentionally designed or unintentionally inherited.

---

## 02. Deliver Value
*Building repeatable value creation*

This is the work itself. The products you build, the services you run, the projects you deliver. Most teams have a project they still point to as their best work, and the uncomfortable question is whether they could repeat it on demand or whether it came down to one person having a very good few months. When results depend on who is staffed, similar projects turn out differently, every team invents its own way of working, and good ideas rarely survive contact with delivery.

Deliver Value is built from five connected systems that follow the work from beginning to end, covering what customers actually need, how work gets coordinated, how solutions get built, how operations run, and how all of it improves. What you get back is consistency. Fewer surprises for clients, less variation between teams, and successful approaches that spread instead of staying with the person who invented them.

**Core principle:** Organizations become more valuable not by repeating work, but by repeating the capability to create value.

---

## 03. Manage Information
*Creating organizational memory*

Every project leaves a trail of research, decisions, files, and data. Most of it gets saved somewhere. Very little gets found again by the person who needs it eight months later. So someone repeats research a colleague already did, three versions of the same document exist and nobody knows which is current, and the reasoning behind a client decision walks out the door with the person who made it.

Manage Information is built from five connected systems that turn scattered material into memory the whole organization can use. Between them they capture what happens, turn it into usable knowledge, keep reusable resources in order, and maintain data people can trust. Done well, this is one of the few things in a business that gets more valuable every year, because you build on what you already know instead of paying to learn it twice.

**Core principle:** Organizations learn when information becomes shared organizational memory rather than individual experience.

---

## 04. Manage the Organization
*Establishing organizational accountability*

At some point running the company stops being something the founders can hold in their heads. People, responsibilities, money, and decision rights multiply faster than anyone expects. Responsibilities start to overlap, the same kind of decision goes one way in one team and another way elsewhere, budgets follow whoever asks loudest, and everything important routes back through the same two people.

Manage the Organization turns "Maria handles that" into something the organization owns. It is built from four connected systems covering who is responsible for what, how decisions and governance work, how money and resources are managed, and how relationships with clients and partners are handled. The result is a company that can coordinate the people delivering the work and put resources behind real priorities without a bottleneck at the top.

**Core principle:** Organizations become stronger when accountability is shared through clear organizational structures rather than carried by individual people.

---

## 05. Communicate
*Creating shared understanding*

Everything above is invisible until someone says it out loud. You can have sharp direction, excellent delivery, and clean accountability, and get very little credit for any of it if nobody outside your head knows. Without a shared approach, the website says one thing and the proposal says another, published pages quietly go out of date, clients keep asking questions you have already answered, and your own team is unsure what the current priorities are.

Communicate is built from three connected systems. One sets how official content gets created, reviewed, and kept current. One organizes it into a digital experience where people can find what they need. One extends it through ongoing conversation. Content made in the first becomes trusted information in the second and reaches people through the third, so the organization sounds like itself everywhere people meet it.

**Core principle:** Communication creates value when it produces shared understanding that enables people to make informed decisions and take meaningful action.

---

## 06. Measure & Improve
*Enabling continuous learning*

Your work already produces evidence. Project outcomes, quality issues, client reactions, the things everyone agreed never to do again. Most of it goes unused. Without a habit of looking back, standards slip slowly enough that nobody notices, the same mistake shows up on a third project, and improvements launched with real enthusiasm get abandoned two months later with no way to say whether they worked.

Measure & Improve is built from three connected systems that measure performance, evaluate quality and outcomes, and turn what you find into changes that stick. They run as a loop rather than a project, so every improvement produces new evidence for the next round. This capability does not close the framework. It restarts it, feeding what you learn back into your foundation, your delivery, your records, and everything else.

**Core principle:** Organizations improve most effectively when every experience becomes evidence, every measurement becomes insight, and every insight leads to meaningful improvement.
