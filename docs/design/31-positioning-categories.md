    - 11 to 13 years: Early Adolescence
    - 13 to 16 years: Mid Adolescence
    - 16 to 18 years: Late Adolescence

  - 18 to 25: Young
  - 25 to 40: Established
  - 40 to 55: Peak
  - 55 to 65: Mature
- **65 and over: Seniors**
  - 65 to 74: Active Retirees
  - 74 and over: Dependent seniors

These are the **Fully Autonomous Buyers** (Working Adults and Active Retirees, 18 to 74) of the Direct Buyers block, as the owner first wrote it:

- **System traits:** full legal capacity; holds credit, debit or bank accounts; enters into long-term contracts.

**Semi-Autonomous Micro-Buyers** (Late Adolescents, 16 to 18), **kept as a buyer category** (owner, 2026-10-27, the block given again):

- **System traits:** age-restricted purchasing power; operates primarily via cash, digital wallets, or gift cards; legally restricted from subscription contracts or credit lines.

So the buyer side has **Micro-Buyers (16 to 18)** and then the five stages from 18 to 74 above. The user stage Late Adolescence (16 to 18) is the user stage of the Micro-Buyers.

Not buyer stages (nothing stated): Minors under 16 (Baby, Child, and Early and Mid Adolescence), and Dependent seniors (74 and over). They appear as **users** (1), and as the people a Proxy Buyer buys for.

## 3. Accessibility and user needs (dynamic cross-sections)

- **By duration**
  - Temporary needs (short-term, acute recovery, situational)
  - Permanent needs (long-term, chronic, stable, or progressive conditions)
- **By functional category**
  - Physical and mobility needs (wheelchair users, motor coordination, fine motor limitations)
  - Sensory needs (visual impairments, hearing loss, sensory processing sensitivities)
  - Cognitive and neurodivergent needs (ADHD, autism, learning differences, age-related cognitive slowing)

## 4. Caregiving and family life roles (universal cross-sections)

- **By dependent category** (who are they caring for?)
  - Childcare provider (parent, legal guardian, or foster parent of minors)
  - Eldercare provider (caring for aging parents, grandparents, or senior relatives)
  - Specialized care provider (caring for a spouse, sibling, or child with permanent or temporary medical or disability needs)
- **By role intensity and structure**
  - Sole / primary caregiver (the main person responsible for daily survival and decisions)
  - Co-caregiver / secondary support (shares the load with a partner, family network, or professional aides)
  - The "sandwich generation" caregiver (simultaneously managing multi-generational care, for example children and elders)
- **By household and legal dynamic**
  - Single-parent / solo-caregiver household (no co-parent or co-caregiver present in the home)
  - Multigenerational household (living with extended family where care is structurally distributed)
  - Kinship caregiver / youth caregiver (minors or young relatives acting as heads of household or primary supports)

## 5. Buyer categories

1. **Direct Buyers** (purchasing exclusively for their own personal use)
2. **Proxy Buyers** (purchasing exclusively on behalf of someone else)
3. **Dual-Intent Buyers** (purchasing a single offering to be used by themselves and their dependents)

## 7. Proposed offering purpose taxonomy

1. **Learning and Development.** Purpose: acquire knowledge, develop skills, or support personal, academic or professional growth. Examples: educational toys, courses, tutoring, employee training, professional development.
2. **Health, Care and Wellbeing.** Purpose: maintain or improve physical, mental or social wellbeing, or provide care and assistance. Examples: care services, accessibility equipment, fitness programmes, wellbeing support, assisted-living services.
3. **Gifting and Appreciation.** Purpose: express affection, gratitude, recognition, celebration or congratulations. Examples: personalised gifts, gift vouchers, employee rewards, corporate gifts, celebration packages.
4. **Entertainment and Enjoyment.** Purpose: provide pleasure, amusement, recreation or enjoyable experiences. Examples: games, films, toys, events, creative workshops, recreational subscriptions.
5. **Social Connection and Relationships.** Purpose: build, maintain or strengthen relationships and shared experiences. Examples: couples' activities, family experiences, community platforms, team-building events.
6. **Productivity and Efficiency.** Purpose: complete tasks more effectively, save time, or improve the use of resources. Examples: productivity software, workflow automation, business tools, administrative services.
7. **Business Growth and Performance.** Purpose: increase revenue, reach customers, improve competitiveness or strengthen organisational performance. Examples: marketing services, business consultancy, sales platforms, analytics, growth programmes.
8. **Creation and Expression.** Purpose: create, customise, communicate or express ideas, identity and creativity. Examples: design tools, art materials, custom products, creative software, branding services.
9. **Everyday Needs and Convenience.** Purpose: meet routine needs, reduce effort or make daily activities easier. Examples: household products, meal delivery, cleaning services, transport, everyday subscriptions.
10. **Protection and Risk Reduction.** Purpose: prevent harm, reduce uncertainty, protect assets or improve security. Examples: insurance, cybersecurity, safety equipment, backup services, childproofing products.
11. **Compliance and Obligations.** Purpose: meet legal, regulatory, contractual or institutional requirements. Examples: tax services, regulatory reporting, certification, mandatory training, licensing support.
12. **Access and Social Support.** Purpose: enable access to resources, opportunities or services that people or organisations might otherwise lack. Examples: scholarships, subsidised services, community support, accessibility solutions, employment assistance.

The groups are the same intervals joined: Baby [0, 3), Child [3, 11), Teenager [11, 18), Minors [0, 18), Working Adult [18, 65), Seniors [65, no limit). The **buyer stages** are the same names over [18, 74) and the Micro-Buyers over [16, 18) (section 2). A person aged exactly 74 is a Dependent senior and so is **not** in a buyer stage: **confirmed by the owner on 2026-10-27**; it is the one place the edge rule changes who counts as a buyer.

**The term leads, the age defines it (owner's emphasis, 2026-10-27: "emphasis on the term than the age").** What the app shows, asks and stores is the **stage term** (Infancy, Early Childhood, Teenager, Established, Peak, Mature, Active Retirees and the others), **not a number of years**. The ages and the edge rule above are the **definition behind the term**: they decide which term a given age gets (and, for a date of birth, the app works the term out), and they are shown **as supporting detail** (for example in the "i" of the term), never as the thing the user has to choose or read first. A question about the user or the buyer therefore offers the terms as its options, in the owner's names and order. **Exception (owner, 2026-10-27): the laws of each country that state a specific limit.** Where a law names an age (a minimum age to buy alcohol, the age of online consent, the age of majority, the minimum working age and so on, 9A and doc 30), the **number is the law's own and is shown and applied as the law states it**, for that country, even where it falls inside a stage or between two stages (for example 13 inside Mid Adolescence, 19 inside Young). The term still names the stage; the legal age is a separate, country-specific fact that the app looks up automatically (below) and states in years, with its source and status. *(This replaces the assistant's earlier reading of the emphasis, "age alone places a person in a stage", which stressed the age; the owner asked for the opposite emphasis.)*

**2. The country's law is applied automatically.** The stages are the same in every country; **what the law does at an age is looked up by the app from the customers' country** (the Organization's country, or the Segment's main country, G3) and never typed by the user. This is the doc 28 principle "fill in what is known". What follows, as design requirements (not built):

- a **table of legal ages by country** inside the app, one row for each rule of 9A (age of majority, online consent, alcohol, tobacco and vapes, credit, employment, and the activities of doc 30), each with its **status** (opened, excerpt, not found) and its source, kept up to date like the jurisdiction profiles and the classifications;
- a rule is **shown or applied only from its status**: an opened or excerpt rule drives a note or a Review check, "not found" says "check the rule for your area" and drives nothing (doc 28, 10.3);
- for a country outside the six, the stages and the plain lists stand and the legal ages say "check the law of that country";
- where a country's law varies inside it (Canada by province, the USA and Australia by state), the app takes the **region of the Organization** where it is known, else the strictest age found and says so;
- a rule can be **dated** (the UK's tobacco age for people born from 2009, Ireland's 21 from 2028, Australia's social media age from 10 Dec 2025): the app uses the date it applies from.

### 1. Proxy Buyer and Dual-Intent Buyer, as defined by the owner's text

The single test is: **does the buyer personally consume the offering, or only pay for it?**

| | Proxy Buyer | Dual-Intent Buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Consumption | Pays only; the whole offering is used by someone else | Buys one offering that serves the buyer's own need **and** a dependent's |

## 9E. Decisions on the three observations of 9D (owner's pasted answer, 2026-10-27)

The owner pasted an answer that agrees with all three proposals. Recorded as decisions:

1. **Relationship and buyer type: agreed.** A relationship (Family member, Business partner) never fixes the buyer type. It is a **context and a suggestion** only. The type comes from **who consumes the offering**: if the buyer is not among the users, the buyer is a **Proxy Buyer**; if the buyer is one of the users, the buyer is a **Dual-Intent Buyer**. The same relationship can therefore give either type (a sibling's single seat is Proxy; a shared family tier used by both is Dual-Intent).
2. **Caregiver override: stays out for now.** It is deferred to the legal rules of each country. The data-separation principle stays (a buyer does not see the end user's behavioural data). The pasted answer names HIPAA (US) and GDPR (EU) as the reasons: HIPAA needing documented authority such as a power of attorney or guardianship, and GDPR needing verifiable consent before health data reaches a third party. **Checked on 2026-10-27:** (a) GDPR Article 9 (gdpr-info.eu, opened): health data is a special category and is barred unless an Article 9(2) exception applies, the main one being explicit consent; Member States may add limits. The pasted statement holds, except that consent is one of several exceptions (vital interests, medical care and public health are others). (b) HIPAA (HHS pages reached only through a search summary; the hhs.gov page itself refused the reader): a power of attorney is **not strictly required**. What matters is whether state law gives the caregiver authority to make health care decisions (a personal representative); a health care power of attorney in effect usually does; family involved in care may still receive relevant information under 45 CFR 164.510(b). The pasted statement is therefore **too strong** and is corrected here. Both are US and EU law and say nothing about the other four countries; the deferral to each country's law stands.
3. **Age bands: agreed.** The stage terms and their edges are the only bands used; the collective phrases of the purposes text are read as groups of stages:
   - "Teenagers (11 to 18)" = Early Adolescence (from 11 up to 13), Mid Adolescence (from 13 up to 16), Late Adolescence (from 16 up to 18).
   - "Working Adults (18 to 65)" = Young (from 18 up to 25), Established (from 25 up to 40), Peak (from 40 up to 55), Mature (from 55 up to 65).
   - An event tied to an age (for example a payment option at 16) is tied to the stage edge, so it fires exactly there.
