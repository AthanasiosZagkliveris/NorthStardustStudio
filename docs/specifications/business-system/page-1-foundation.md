# 1\. Customer Segments and Profiles

### Column Instructions

* Divide your Target Audience into 1 primary segment (maximum 2), focusing on the highest-value or easiest-to-reach groups.
* Define up to 3 customer profiles, including their problems, needs, and the best approach for reaching them.

### Customer Segment Inputs

Create a nested card for each **Customer Segment**.

**A. Name**

**B. Description - i**

The information section must present all the parameters the user should take into account:

**Parameters:**

a. Demographics: What are their age range, education level, and income range?  
b. Geographics: Where do they live, work or operate?  
c. Business Data: If you sell to companies, what is their company type, size and industry?  
d. Purchasing Power \& Behavior: What influences how much they spend, when they buy, and whether it is worthwhile?
e. Market Size: How large is this group?

### Customer Profile Inputs

Customer Profiles must appear **nested under their Customer Segment**.

By default, at least **1 Customer Profile card** should exist.

Allow the user to add additional profiles using **+ ADD**, up to the defined maximum of 3 profiles.

**A. Name**

**B. Description - i**

The information section must present all the parameters the user should take into account:

**Parameters:**

a. Specific Personal Information: What is his exact age, gender, marital status, education, and Job title?  
b. Psychographics: What does he value, believe in, enjoy, and consider important?

c. Social \& Interest Hubs: Which communities, professional networks, events and other places does he regularly visit or participate in?
d. Information \& Shopping Habits: How does he find, learn about, compare and buy products or services?

**C. Goals \& Desires:** What does he wants to achieve, and what would his ideal outcome be?

**D. Challenges \& Problems:** What frustrates him, wastes his time or money, or causes stress?

**E. Objections:** What might stop him from buying your product?

**F. Needs - i**

The information section must present all the parameters the user should take into account:

**Parameters:**

a. Functional Needs: The practical task he needs to accomplish.  
b. Emotional Needs: How does he wants to feel when using the solution.  
c. Social Needs: How does he wants others to see him.

# 2\. Competitors and Alternatives

### Column Instructions

* Select 3–5 competitors and analyse them for each Customer Segment.
* Focus on a relevant product line, pricing tier, or geography.

### Competitors and Alternatives Inputs

Create a section for each competitor or alternative.

**A. Name**

**B. Type - i**

Provide a **drop down selection menu** containing the following Types and explanations:

a. Direct competitors - Offer a similar solution to the same need in your area.  
b. Indirect competitor - Offer different solution but solve the same customer problem.  
c. Substitutes - Offer alternative ways to satisfy the exact same customer need.  
d. Internal solutions - The customer solves the need himself.  
e. Status quo - The customer does nothing and continues with the current situation.

**C. Description - i**

The information section must present all the parameters the user should take into account:

**Parameters:**

a. What do they sell or provide?  
b. Which areas and industries do they serve?  
c. How do they reach and attract customers?  
d. What do customers say about their experience?

**D. Competitors SWOT Analysis**

**I. Strengths - i** - What does the competitor do particularly well?  
**II. Weaknesses - i** - Where does the competitor fall short?  
**III. Opportunities - i** - What changes in the market could benefit the competitor?  
**IV. Threats - i** - What could weaken the competitor's position?

**E. Prioritise Insights and Differentiate - i**

* What can you learn from competitors to make your offering more distinctive?

Use **+ ADD** to add competitors and alternatives.

# 3\. Value Proposition

### Column Instructions

* Define a clear, concise Value Proposition for each Customer Segment.
* State the specific problem you solve and the key benefits you provide.
* Explain what makes your offering different or better than competitors.

### Value Proposition Input

Use an **Empty Space - i** as the input area.

The information section must state:

* Identify how the Value Proposition addresses each Customer Profile’s emotional and social needs.

The Value Proposition should be associated with the relevant Customer Segment.

\---

# 4\. Offerings

### Column Instructions

* Turn customer problems and operational needs into practical product features and solutions.
* Ensure every feature supports the Value Proposition.

### Offerings Inputs

Create a section for each offering.

**A. Name**

**B. Needs Adressed**

Create a **drop down selection menu** populated with the Needs from the Customer Profile.

* More than 1 need can be selected.

**C. Category/Format**

Create a **drop down selection menu** with:

a. Product  
b. Service  
c. Hybrid  
d. Other

**D. Description - i**

The information section must present all the parameters the user should take into account:

**Parameters:**

a. Function: What does it do, and what result should it deliver? 
b. Customer Benefit — What problem does it solve, or what does the customer gain or achieve?  
c. Content \& Scope — What does it include, and what are its boundaries?
d. Purchase \& Delivery — How does the customer purchase, pay for, and receive the offering? 
e. Support \& Communication — What support and communication does the customer receive? 
f. Requirements \& Conditions — What does the customer need to provide or have, and what conditions apply?

Use **+ ADD** to add offerings.
